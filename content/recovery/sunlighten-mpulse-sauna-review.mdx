## Why Full-Spectrum Infrared Is the Selling Point

Most infrared saunas emit only far-infrared radiation — the wavelengths that generate heat and sweating. The Sunlighten mPulse Believe adds near-infrared and mid-infrared heaters to the far-infrared base, creating a full-spectrum system that targets different biological mechanisms simultaneously.

Understanding why this matters requires understanding what each wavelength does:

**Near-infrared (700–1400nm):**
The same wavelengths used in red light therapy panels. Near-IR penetrates tissue to 3–5cm depth, where it stimulates cytochrome c oxidase in the mitochondria, increasing ATP production and triggering cellular repair mechanisms. This is photobiomodulation occurring at sauna temperatures — a dual stimulus unavailable in conventional or far-IR-only saunas.

**Mid-infrared (1400–3000nm):**
Penetrates soft tissue to approximately 2–3cm depth. Most strongly associated with pain reduction and inflammation — mid-IR activates nitric oxide release, improving circulation and reducing inflammatory mediator concentrations. RCTs show significant reduction in fibromyalgia pain and cardiovascular improvement with mid-IR exposure.

**Far-infrared (3000nm and above):**
The heat generator. Far-IR is absorbed primarily by the skin surface and subcutaneous tissue, raising core body temperature — the mechanism behind the cardiovascular and detoxification benefits of sauna use. The KIHD study data showing 50% reduction in cardiovascular mortality with 4–7 sessions per week of sauna use is based on high-temperature traditional saunas, but far-IR achieves comparable core temperature elevation at lower ambient temperatures.

Sunlighten states that the mPulse Believe delivers all three.

## The 7 Programmable Protocols

The mPulse's built-in tablet runs Sunlighten's protocol system — pre-programmed sessions targeting specific health outcomes, each with a different infrared spectrum weighting and temperature curve:

**1. Detoxification Protocol (130°F, 37 min)**
Far-IR dominant. Maximises core temperature elevation for sweat-based toxin elimination. Raises core temperature approximately 2–3°F — the threshold associated with heavy metal and lipophilic compound excretion in clinical literature.

**2. Cardiovascular Protocol (127°F, 45 min)**
Combination spectrum. Designed to replicate the cardiovascular stimulus from the Finnish sauna studies. Heart rate reaches 120–140 BPM in most users — equivalent to moderate-intensity aerobic exercise. The Laukkanen et al. (2018) data published in Mayo Clinic Proceedings shows dose-dependent cardiovascular mortality reduction with increasing sauna frequency.

**3. Anti-Aging Protocol (127°F, 30 min)**
Near and mid-IR dominant. Targets collagen synthesis, cellular repair, and mitochondrial function. The protocol most analogous to red light therapy benefit — effectively combining a 30-minute full-body red light therapy session with a sauna session simultaneously.

**4. Weight Management Protocol (127°F, 30 min)**
Combination. Increases caloric expenditure via thermogenesis — burns approximately 300–600 calories per session depending on individual metabolic response and cardiovascular fitness.

**5. Pain Relief Protocol (127°F, 30 min)**
Mid-IR heavy. Nitric oxide release reduces pain and inflammation. Best-evidenced for joint pain and fibromyalgia — the Matsushita et al. (2008) study showed 13 of 14 fibromyalgia patients achieved significant symptom reduction with infrared sauna therapy over 6 weeks.

**6. Relaxation Protocol (127°F, 40 min)**
Far-IR at moderate intensity. Parasympathetic activation, cortisol reduction, sleep quality improvement. This protocol is best scheduled in the late afternoon — the 2–3 hour cool-down window positions peak melatonin rise at optimal sleep onset.

**7. Athlete's Recovery Protocol (130°F, 30 min)**
Full-spectrum. Combines muscle repair (near-IR), inflammation reduction (mid-IR), and cardiovascular flushing (far-IR). Used within 2 hours post-training, this protocol activates the same pathways that have made post-exercise sauna a standard practice in professional athletic recovery programmes.
