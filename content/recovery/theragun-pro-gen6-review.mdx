## The Amplitude Specification

Theragun is a widely known brand, and the Pro's 16mm amplitude is the highest of the devices compared on this site.

**Amplitude** is the total stroke length of the percussive head. At 16mm, the Theragun Pro moves its head 16 millimetres with each percussion. At 12mm (Hypervolt 2 Pro) or 10mm (budget devices), the stroke is shorter, which is generally expected to reach less deeply.

This matters because the physiological effects of percussive therapy — increased local blood flow, reduced muscle tone via Golgi tendon organ inhibition, pain gate modulation — are proposed mechanisms, and evidence that a longer stroke gives better results is limited. As a rule of thumb, shorter amplitudes are commonly considered adequate for superficial muscles and longer amplitudes are marketed for deep tissue.

---

## Stall Force: Why 60 Lbs Matters

**Stall force** is the amount of pressure the motor can sustain before the percussion rate drops significantly. This is the specification that matters for practical use.

A lower stall force means the device may slow when pressed firmly into a large muscle. A higher stall force, such as the 60 lbs listed for the Theragun Pro, is intended to let the device keep its speed under firmer pressure. Stall force figures are manufacturer-stated and measured in different ways by different brands.

## The Rotating Arm: Solving the Self-Treatment Problem

The Theragun Pro's defining design innovation is its rotating arm — the attachment point between the handle and the motor head rotates through 4 preset angles. This solves a fundamental problem with percussive therapy self-treatment: getting to your own back, posterior shoulder, and hip musculature.

**Arm positions:**
- **0° (straight):** Standard use, front of body, legs
- **30°:** Slight angle for shoulder and upper back
- **60°:** Mid-back reach
- **90° (perpendicular):** Full back, glutes, and posterior chain access without contortion

The 90° position is intended to make self-treatment of the mid-back and glutes easier, areas that are otherwise hard to reach. Whether this justifies a premium over fixed-arm devices depends on the user.

## The Science of Percussive Therapy

The mechanisms below are the proposed explanations for how percussive therapy may work; evidence for each in humans varies.

**Vibration and muscle tone reduction:**
Rapid percussion activates the Golgi tendon organ (GTO) — a mechanoreceptor at the muscle-tendon junction that responds to sudden tension. GTO activation triggers an autogenic inhibition reflex, reducing muscle tone. This is the same mechanism targeted in contract-relax stretching and proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation (PNF).

It is unclear how much amplitude is needed to activate the GTO, and we are not aware of published evidence that a 16mm stroke is required.

**Local blood flow and metabolic waste clearance:**
Mechanical percussion is proposed to increase local blood flow, which may help clear metabolic waste products associated with delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS). The size of this effect in published studies is variable.

**Pain gate modulation:**
High-frequency mechanical stimulation activates large-diameter A-beta sensory fibres, which competitively inhibit nociceptive (pain) signals travelling via smaller C fibres — the same mechanism as rubbing a bruise instinctively reduces pain. High-frequency percussion is proposed to provide A-beta activation, which may temporarily reduce pain perception in treated areas.

## Attachment Heads: Six Included

The Theragun Pro Gen 6 ships with six attachment heads for different applications:

**Standard ball:** Large muscle groups (quads, hamstrings, glutes, chest). Most frequently used.

**Dampener:** Sensitive areas, joints, near bone. Softer material reduces percussion intensity.

**Thumb:** Targeted trigger point work, feet, palms, forearms.

**Cone:** Precise trigger point, small muscle groups (peroneals, forearm flexors, tibialis posterior).

**Wedge:** IT band, shoulder blades, muscle scraping technique (fascial release).

**Supersoft:** Extremely sensitive areas, post-surgical recovery, bruised tissue.

## Battery Life and Charging

The Theragun Pro Gen 6 includes **two swappable batteries**, each providing approximately 150 minutes of use. The manufacturer lists a total run time of 300 minutes across both batteries, which is intended for professional clinical use.

The batteries are swappable, which matters more in clinical settings where devices are used continuously.

## Noise: The Main Practical Limitation

Percussive devices are generally loud at high speed, and the Theragun Pro is commonly described as louder than the Hypervolt 2 Pro, which the manufacturer lists at 55 dB. We have not measured either, and brands measure noise differently.

For use in a private space, noise may not matter. In shared spaces (offices, hotel rooms, early morning sessions while others sleep), it is a consideration. The Hypervolt 2 Pro or a compact Theragun Mini would serve better in these contexts.
