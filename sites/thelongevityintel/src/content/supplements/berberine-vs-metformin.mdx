**The short answer:** the only head-to-head trial we found is a 36-person, 3-month pilot in newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes, in which berberine and metformin lowered glucose to a similar degree. That is not proof they are interchangeable. Metformin is a prescription drug with decades of outcome data; berberine is a supplement without such data. Neither is proven to extend life. Do not swap a prescribed medicine for a supplement without your doctor.

## What the evidence shows

| Question | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Berberine vs metformin, glucose | [Yin 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.metabol.2008.01.013), study A: 36 adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes randomised to berberine or metformin, 3 months; authors reported a similar effect | Pilot; short; diabetes only |
| Berberine, broader evidence | [Meta-analysis of 46 RCTs](https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/2074610) in type 2 diabetes: lower HbA1c, fasting and postprandial glucose | Trial quality varies |
| Metformin and survival | [Bannister 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.12354), UK primary-care records: 78,241 people on metformin monotherapy; median survival in matched people without diabetes was 15% lower than in the metformin group (survival time ratio 0.85) | Observational; prone to selection bias; not a trial |

The metformin survival paper is often quoted in longevity circles. It shows an association in patients who were chosen to take metformin, not that metformin extends life in healthy people. ## The boundary

Metformin needs a prescription and monitoring (for example kidney function). Berberine is sold as a supplement, is not regulated as a drug, and can interact with medicines. If you have diabetes or high blood sugar, the right step is a doctor, not a substitute.
