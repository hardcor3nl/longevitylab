**The short answer:** for healthy adults under 75, the 2024 Endocrine Society guideline suggests against taking vitamin D above the standard recommended intake to prevent disease; it suggests supplementing for children, people over 75, pregnancy and high-risk prediabetes. The largest trial, VITAL, found no reduction in cancer or major cardiovascular events. Evidence for vitamin K2 is smaller still, and vitamin K interacts with warfarin. Talk to your doctor before starting either, and before taking more than the standard amounts.

## What the evidence shows

Grades follow the [rubric](/database#rubric).

| Question | Grade | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vitamin D corrects deficiency and prevents rickets | Strong (established indication) | The [Endocrine Society guideline](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgae290) suggests empiric vitamin D for ages 1 to 18 to prevent nutritional rickets. | A guideline for people without established indications; it does not replace standard intake recommendations. |
| Vitamin D prevents cancer or heart disease in adults | Weak | [VITAL](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1809944), 25,871 adults on 2,000 IU/day for 5.3 years: cancer HR 0.96, major cardiovascular events HR 0.97, neither significant. | Most participants were not deficient, so it says little about deficient people. |
| Vitamin D lowers mortality | Weak, mixed | A [meta-analysis of 52 RCTs](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4673) (75,454 people): no effect on all-cause mortality (RR 0.98), but cancer death fell 16%. The Endocrine Society notes a potential mortality benefit at 75 and older. | Cancer-death finding is secondary; guideline suggestion is for over-75s only. |
| Vitamin K2 (MK-7) and arterial stiffness | Emerging | One [3-year RCT of 244 postmenopausal women](https://doi.org/10.1160/TH14-08-0675) found lower arterial stiffness measures. | A single trial; an author works with the MK-7 supplier VitaK. |
| Vitamin K2 and bone | Emerging | A [meta-analysis of 16 RCTs](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2022.979649) (6,425 women) found better lumbar spine density; the overall fracture effect was not significant (RR 0.96) until one heterogeneous study was excluded. | Postmenopausal women with osteoporosis; results depend on excluding a study. |
| D3 plus K2 together beats D3 alone | Not shown | We did not find a trial comparing them for hard outcomes. | The case for combining rests on mechanism, not proof. |

## Testing and doses

The Endocrine Society found no clinical-trial evidence to support routine 25(OH)D screening in the general population and suggests against routine testing without an established indication; it also found no clear optimal blood level for disease prevention. It notes trial doses varied a great deal and that optimal doses remain unclear. The NHS advises adults need 10 micrograms (400 IU) a day and to avoid more than 100 micrograms (4,000 IU) a day. This page does not give a personal dose; that is a question for your doctor, informed by a test if you have a reason to have one.

## Safety

- **Too much vitamin D** over a long time can cause hypercalcaemia, per the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/).
- **Vitamin K and warfarin.** A [prospective controlled trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15543329/) found over-the-counter vitamin K1-containing multivitamins disrupted warfarin anticoagulation in patients with low vitamin K status. If you take warfarin or another anticoagulant, ask the prescriber before any vitamin K product.
- The NHS says 1 mg or less a day of vitamin K supplements is unlikely to cause harm, and that there is not enough evidence on higher doses.
