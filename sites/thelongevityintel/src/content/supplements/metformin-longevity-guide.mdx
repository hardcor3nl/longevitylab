**The short answer:** metformin is a prescription diabetes drug, and its longevity reputation rests on observational data. In UK primary-care records, people with type 2 diabetes who started metformin had survival that matched or exceeded matched people without diabetes. That cannot show metformin caused it, and it does not apply to healthy people. We found no completed randomised trial of aging outcomes. Talk to your doctor; this page gives no dosing or sourcing.

## What the evidence shows

Grade: Weak for longevity in people without diabetes.

| Item | What was found | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Bannister 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.12354) | 78,241 people on metformin monotherapy, 12,222 on sulphonylurea and 90,463 matched controls without diabetes; adjusted median survival in controls was 15% lower than in the metformin group (survival time ratio 0.85, CI 0.81 to 0.90) | Observational; who receives metformin is not random; people with diabetes only |
| Aging trials | We found no completed randomised trial of metformin for aging outcomes in healthy people | The TAME trial is designed to test this but had not reported in what we read |
| Versus berberine | A [36-person pilot](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.metabol.2008.01.013) found a similar glucose effect in new type 2 diabetes | Pilot; diabetes only |

## The boundary

Metformin is prescribed and monitored, for example for kidney function. Whether it makes sense for someone without diabetes is a medical decision.
