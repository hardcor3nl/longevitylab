**The short answer:** the best-supported use of collagen supplements is skin. A 2020 systematic review of 10 publications found oral collagen improved measures of skin health, with almost all studies reporting benefit. That review focused on mechanisms rather than trial quality, and short studies are common. We did not verify evidence for joints, bones or longevity, so we make no such claim. Talk to your doctor if you have allergies to fish, shellfish or eggs.

## What the evidence shows

Grade: Emerging (skin only).

| Question | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | [Systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.13435): of 6,531 screened publications, 10 included; oral intact or hydrolysed collagen improved skin health parameters; three mechanisms proposed (effects on fibroblasts, M2-like macrophages, oral tolerance) | Mechanistic review of few studies; quality and funding not summarised in the abstract |
| Joints, bone, longevity | Not assessed here | No claim made |

## Larger reviews of skin trials

Three later reviews put numbers on the skin evidence, and each flags its limits.

| Review | What it found | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Nutrients 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15092080): 26 RCTs, 1,721 people | Hydrolysed collagen improved skin hydration and elasticity against placebo; hydration effects varied with collagen source and duration | The authors identified several biases in the included trials |
| [Int J Dermatol 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33742704/): 19 double-blind RCTs, 1,125 people (95% women) | Better hydration, elasticity and wrinkles than placebo; authors concluded 90 days was effective | Abstract reports pooled favourable results but no risk-of-bias grading or heterogeneity figure; 95% of participants were women |
| [Eur J Clin Nutr 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42342959/): 25 RCTs (13 to 236 people) | Hydration improved in 10 of 15 trials, elasticity in 10 of 13, wrinkle depth or volume in 9 of 10; generally well tolerated, low adverse events | Most trials had a high risk of bias; longer, better-powered RCTs needed |

So the skin signal is consistent but the trials are weak. We found no verified evidence here for joints, bone or longevity.

## Who it suits, who should skip it

**Reasonable to try if** you want to test a skin benefit, accept it is modest and trial-dependent, and will judge it over about 90 days (the period one review concluded was effective).

**Skip it if** you have a fish, shellfish or egg allergy (check the source), you want it for joints, bones or longevity (not shown here), or you expect more than the small trials found.
