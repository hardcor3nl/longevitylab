**The short answer:** NMN is lawful to sell as a US dietary supplement: the FDA reversed its 2022 exclusion on 29 September 2025. Human trials show NMN raises blood NAD+ and is well tolerated over weeks to months, but the benefits on strength, glucose and other outcomes are small or mixed, and nothing shows it extends life. NR (nicotinamide riboside), the other popular NAD+ precursor, has similar mixed results and no head-to-head trial against NMN that we could find. Talk to your doctor before starting either.

## Is NMN legal? The FDA timeline

| Date | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Nov 2022 | FDA said NMN was excluded from the dietary supplement definition because it had been authorised for drug investigation first (per the letters as reported) | Reported in NPA and trade-press summaries; not read at FDA source |
| Mar 2023 and Aug 2024 | Natural Products Association and Alliance for Natural Health filed a citizen petition, then NPA sued | NPA |
| 29 Sept 2025 | FDA concluded NMN is not excluded from the definition of a dietary supplement, citing evidence it was marketed as a supplement in the US before its drug authorisation ([FDA document](https://www.regulations.gov/document/FDA-2023-P-0872-2754)) | Venable, NPA |
| 2 Dec 2025 | FDA letter reinstating a previously issued new dietary ingredient acknowledgment ([FDA document](https://www.regulations.gov/document/FDA-2022-S-0023-0067)) | NPA |

Two caveats. FDA does not approve supplements or their claims, and NMN is still a new dietary ingredient, so companies must meet notification requirements; Venable also notes firms cannot rely on self-affirmed GRAS status to skip them. And rules differ outside the US: check your own regulator. We could not open the regulations.gov documents with our tools, so the timeline above rests on the NPA and Venable accounts of them, with the FDA links provided for you to read; FDA's [ingredient information page](https://www.fda.gov/food/dietary-supplements/information-select-dietary-supplement-ingredients-and-other-substances), updated 1 May 2026, did not surface an NMN entry to our reader. We found nothing later than December 2025.

## NMN vs NR: what trials show

Grades follow the [rubric](/database#rubric). Doses are what the trial used, not a recommendation.

| | NMN | NR |
|---|---|---|
| Grade | Emerging | Weak to Emerging |
| Raises NAD+ measures in blood | Yes: [Igarashi 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-022-00084-z) (250 mg/day, older men, 6 or 12 weeks) and [Yi 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-022-00705-1) (80 adults, 300, 600 or 900 mg/day for 60 days, highest at 600 and 900) | Reported consistently in reviews |
| Functional signals | Igarashi: only nominal gains in gait speed and left grip, no change in body composition. Yi: longer six-minute walk and better blood biological-age readout vs placebo, no HOMA-IR change. [Yoshino 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abe9985): better muscle insulin sensitivity in postmenopausal women with prediabetes (250 mg/day, 10 weeks) | [Dollerup 2019](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2019-01081): no effect on glucose tolerance or insulin in men with obesity (2,000 mg/day, 12 weeks) |
| Other | Yi authors declare employment at Abinopharm and ABA Chemicals; Yoshino's authors disclosed patent-licensing ties | [Jensen 2022](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.158314): NR plus pterostilbene (1,000 mg plus 200 mg/day) in 32 adults aged 55 to 80 did not improve muscle stem-cell recruitment or recovery after induced muscle injury |
| Muscle in adults over 60 | A [2025 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcsm.13799) found no significant effect on muscle mass, grip or gait speed | Same review: not supportive; mixed results in special groups |
| Overall | A [2026 systematic review of 33 human studies](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arr.2026.103057): oral NR and NMN consistently raise NAD-related blood levels and are generally well tolerated, but functional and healthspan outcomes were heterogeneous and often null; clinical effectiveness for anti-aging is inconclusive | Same |

Two things to note. Rising NAD+ in blood is a marker, not a health outcome. And no head-to-head trial of NMN against NR appeared in our search, so any claim that one "beats" the other is not trial-based. The site's [NAD+ boosters guide](/supplements/nad-boosters-complete-guide) covers the biology.
