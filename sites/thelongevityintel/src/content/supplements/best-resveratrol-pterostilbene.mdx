**The short answer:** resveratrol has no human evidence for longer life. In 783 older Italian adults, urinary resveratrol metabolites did not predict mortality, and a meta-analysis of 11 randomised trials found better glucose control only in people with diabetes, not in people without it. We found no trial comparing resveratrol with pterostilbene for outcomes, so claims that one is better rest on absorption arguments, not results. Talk to your doctor before starting.

## What the evidence shows

Grades follow the [rubric](/database#rubric); overall grade: Weak.

| Question | Grade | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mortality | Weak | [Semba 2014](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2014.1582): 783 adults 65+, 268 deaths over 9 years; lowest vs highest quartile of urinary resveratrol metabolites, HR 0.80 (CI 0.54 to 1.17); no association with inflammation, cardiovascular disease or cancer | Dietary exposure, observational |
| Glucose control | Weak, only in diabetes | [11 RCTs, 388 people](https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.113.082024): improvements in people with diabetes; no significant effect in non-diabetic participants | Small trials |
| Pterostilbene | Not shown alone | The only trial we found combined it with NR: [Jensen 2022](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.158314), 32 older adults, NR 1,000 mg plus pterostilbene 200 mg, muscle stem cell response after injury | Combination product; cannot separate the effect of pterostilbene |
