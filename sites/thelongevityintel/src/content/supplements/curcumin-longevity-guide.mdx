**The short answer:** curcumin's best-supported use is arthritis symptoms, and even that is cautious. A 2022 meta-analysis of 29 randomised trials (2,396 people, five types of arthritis) found curcumin or turmeric extract may improve inflammation and pain, but the authors warned the trials were low in quality and few. We found no trial showing a longevity benefit. Talk to your doctor; turmeric products have been linked to serious liver injury in case reports (below), and supplements can interact with medicines, so ask a pharmacist.

## Safety: liver injury reports

A [case series from the US Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2022.09.026) reported ten cases of liver injury linked to turmeric products enrolled between 2004 and 2022, all since 2011 and six since 2017. Eight were women; median age 56; injury was mainly hepatocellular; five were hospitalised and one died of acute liver failure. Turmeric was confirmed in all 7 products tested and 3 also contained piperine (black pepper). Seven of ten patients carried the HLA-B*35:01 gene variant, which is far more common in these cases than in the general population (allele frequency 0.450 against 0.056 to 0.069). Ten cases cannot give a rate, but they are why anyone with liver disease should ask a doctor first.

## Who it suits, who should skip it

**Reasonable to discuss with a doctor if** you have osteoarthritis or another arthritis and want to ask about curcumin alongside treatment, knowing the trials were low quality.

**Skip it, or ask first, if** you have liver disease or have had abnormal liver tests, take prescription medicines (ask a pharmacist about interactions), or want it for longevity: no trial shows that.
