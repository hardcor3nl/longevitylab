**The short answer:** taurine's anti-aging reputation comes from animal studies. A 2023 Science paper found that raising taurine increased health span in mice and monkeys and lifespan in mice. In people, a 2026 meta-analysis of 34 trials found lower fasting glucose, HbA1c and triglycerides, which are markers, not outcomes. Nothing shows taurine lengthens human life. Talk to your doctor before starting, particularly with diabetes or heart medicines.

## What the evidence shows

Grades follow the [rubric](/database#rubric).

| Question | Grade | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lifespan and health span in animals | Not applicable to humans | [Science 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abn9257): circulating taurine declines with age in mice, monkeys and humans; supplementation increased health span in mice and monkeys. Lower taurine in humans correlated with age-related diseases. | Correlation in humans; the authors called for human trials. The lead institution filed patent applications. |
| Cardiometabolic markers in people | Emerging | [Meta-analysis of 34 RCTs](https://doi.org/10.1093/nutrit/nuaf220): fasting glucose -5.90 mg/dL, HbA1c -0.21%, HOMA-IR -0.57, triglycerides -14.42 mg/dL, total cholesterol -12.41 (units as reported). | Markers only; trial dose range 1.5 to 3.0 g/day in one subgroup analysis; quality and duration vary. |
