**The short answer:** oral NMN and NR reliably raise NAD-related levels in blood and are generally well tolerated over weeks to months, but a 2026 systematic review of 33 human studies found that effects on function, metabolism and other health outcomes were heterogeneous and often null. It found no outcome trials of IV NAD+ for anti-aging or wellness. Whether boosting NAD+ improves human health is unproven. Talk to your doctor before starting.

## What the evidence shows

Grades follow the [rubric](/database#rubric); overall grade for oral precursors: Emerging.

| Question | Grade | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raise NAD-related blood levels (oral NMN or NR) | Moderate | The [2026 systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arr.2026.103057): consistent target engagement | A biomarker, not a health outcome |
| Muscle mass and function in adults over 60 | Weak | A [2025 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcsm.13799): current evidence does not support NMN or NR for preserving muscle mass and function | Small trials |
| Insulin sensitivity | Mixed | NMN improved muscle insulin sensitivity in postmenopausal women with prediabetes ([Science 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abe9985)); NR did not change glucose tolerance in men with obesity ([2019](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2019-01081)) | Different populations, precursors and doses |
| IV NAD+ for anti-aging | Not shown | The 2026 review found no eligible outcome trials | No outcome data |
