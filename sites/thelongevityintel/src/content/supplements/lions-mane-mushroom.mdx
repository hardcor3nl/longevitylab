**The short answer:** the human evidence for lion's mane is small. A 2025 systematic review found five randomised trials and three pilot trials; on the Mini-Mental State Examination one RCT and one pilot showed a combined weighted mean increase of 1.17 points in the treated group. Most of the mechanism work is in cells and animals. Nothing shows dementia prevention or longer life. Talk to your doctor before starting.

## What the evidence shows

Grade: Weak to Emerging.

| Question | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|

| Young healthy adults | [Nutrients 2023 pilot](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15224842), 41 adults: quicker Stroop task 60 minutes after a dose; a non-significant trend to lower stress at 28 days. ## What the two young-adult trials found

| Trial | Design | Amount used | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Nutrients 2023 pilot](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15224842) | 41 healthy adults aged 18 to 45, double-blind, parallel groups; acute and 28-day | 1.8 g Hericium erinaceus | Faster Stroop task 60 minutes after one dose (p = 0.005); a trend to lower subjective stress after 28 days (p = 0.051); the authors also saw null findings and call the sample too small |
| [Front Nutr 2025 crossover](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40276537/) | 18 healthy adults aged 18 to 35, double-blind, placebo-controlled | 3 g of a 10:1 fruiting-body extract, one dose | No significant effect on overall cognition or mood 90 minutes later; better pegboard performance only, so any benefit may be task-specific |

Neither trial tested memory decline, dementia or lifespan.

## Who it suits, who should skip it

**Reasonable to discuss with a doctor if** you are curious about a cognitive or mood effect, accept that the trials are small and mixed, and have no mushroom allergy.

**Skip it if** you expect dementia prevention or longer life (not tested), have a mushroom allergy, or take other medicines and have not checked with a pharmacist. See [best nootropics for longevity](/best/best-nootropics-longevity).
