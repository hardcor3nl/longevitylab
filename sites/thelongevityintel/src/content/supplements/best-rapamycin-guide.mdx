**The short answer:** no randomised trial shows rapamycin extends human life. The main human trial in healthy adults, PEARL, found no significant change in its primary endpoint (visceral fat) after 48 weeks of low-dose weekly rapamycin, and its authors are employees and shareholders of AgelessRx, the company that ran it. Rapamycin is a prescription drug and this page gives no dosing or sourcing information. Talk to your doctor.

## What the human evidence shows

Grade: Weak (human evidence for longevity is early and company-run).

| Item | What was found | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| [PEARL trial](https://doi.org/10.18632/aging.206235) | Placebo, 5 mg or 10 mg compounded rapamycin weekly for 48 weeks. Visceral adiposity (primary) did not change significantly. Blood biomarkers stayed within normal ranges. Lean tissue mass and self-reported pain improved in women on 10 mg; emotional well-being and general health improved at 5 mg. Adverse events were similar across groups | Authors are employees and shareholders of AgelessRx; small sample; one year; several secondary findings in subgroups |
| Lifespan in humans | None | Animal results are not human evidence |

## What this page does not do

It does not describe doses, schedules, sourcing or how to get a prescription. Rapamycin suppresses the immune system in higher-dose medical use, and monitoring matters. Anything beyond information belongs in a conversation with a physician who knows your health history.
