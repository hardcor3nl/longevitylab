**The short answer:** the human evidence for longevity peptides is close to non-existent. For BPC-157, the most discussed one, a 2025 systematic review found 36 studies, of which 35 were preclinical (cells and animals) and only one clinical. The review notes it lacks FDA approval and is banned in professional sports. Nothing on this page is a protocol or a sourcing tip. Talk to your doctor.

## What the evidence shows

Grade: Not shown in humans.

| Item | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| BPC-157 | [Systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1177/15563316251355551): 544 articles identified, 36 included, 35 preclinical and 1 clinical; in animal models it improved muscle, tendon, ligament and bone healing outcomes; the one clinical study was retrospective (musculoskeletal pain after intraarticular injection) | Animal results do not establish human benefit or safety |
| Regulatory status | The same review states BPC-157 lacks FDA approval; FDA's [503A bulks page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/bulk-drug-substances-used-compounding-under-section-503a-fdc-act) (updated 14 May 2026) describes the framework but did not name specific peptides in the text we could read | Rules change; check FDA directly |
