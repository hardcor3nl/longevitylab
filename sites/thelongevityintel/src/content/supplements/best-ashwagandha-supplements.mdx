**The short answer:** two meta-analyses found ashwagandha extract lowered anxiety and stress scores (12 trials, 1,002 people) and modestly improved sleep (5 trials, 400 people). But heterogeneity was very high, trials were short and safety data are limited, so the true size of the effect is uncertain. It is not a longevity intervention. Talk to your doctor before taking it, especially with thyroid, liver or autoimmune conditions or pregnancy.

## Safety: rare liver injury

A [case series from Iceland and the US Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network](https://doi.org/10.1111/liv.14393) described five people who developed liver injury attributed to ashwagandha supplements. All had jaundice and symptoms such as nausea, itching and abdominal discomfort after 2 to 12 weeks; the injury was cholestatic or mixed, no one developed liver failure, and liver tests normalised within 1 to 5 months in four of them (one was lost to follow-up). Four of the five were also taking other supplements, and in one case rhodiola was a possible cause alongside ashwagandha; the authors concluded the cases show ashwagandha's hepatotoxic potential. Five cases cannot give a rate, but they are why liver conditions are a reason to ask a doctor first.

## Who it suits, who should skip it

**Reasonable to discuss with a doctor if** you want to try it for stress or sleep, have no liver, thyroid or autoimmune condition, and understand the trials were short and very heterogeneous.

**Skip it, or ask first, if** you have liver disease, are pregnant, have a thyroid or autoimmune condition, or want it for longevity. Non-supplement options are on the [sleep hygiene](/protocols/sleep-hygiene-protocol) and [breathwork](/protocols/breathwork-protocols) pages.
