**The short answer:** berberine lowers HbA1c and fasting glucose in people with type 2 diabetes: a meta-analysis of 46 randomised trials found mean reductions of 0.73 in HbA1c and 0.86 in fasting glucose. We found no trial showing benefit for longevity in people without diabetes. In one pilot, about a third of patients had transient gastrointestinal side effects. It can interact with medicines. Talk to your doctor before taking it, especially alongside diabetes drugs.

## Beyond blood sugar: cholesterol and interactions

- **Cholesterol.** A [2023 meta-analysis of 18 placebo-controlled trials](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36941490/) (1,788 adults, 4 to 24 weeks, 83% in China or Hong Kong) found berberine lowered LDL cholesterol by 0.46 mmol/L, total cholesterol by 0.48 mmol/L and triglycerides by 0.34 mmol/L. Short trials; not a substitute for prescribed lipid treatment.
- **Drug interactions.** A [2026 narrative review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42653808/) says berberine should be treated as an active pharmacological agent, not an inert supplement: it inhibits CYP3A4 and other enzymes and modulates transporters, and in kidney-transplant recipients it raised cyclosporine exposure (AUC up 34.5%). The interactions are documented in humans for only a few drugs and are mechanistic for most.

## Who it suits, who should skip it

**Might be worth discussing with a doctor if** you have type 2 diabetes, are already under care, and want to ask about berberine alongside your treatment. See [berberine vs metformin](/supplements/berberine-vs-metformin).

**Skip it, or ask a pharmacist first, if** you take diabetes drugs, cyclosporine or other narrow-therapeutic-index medicines, are pregnant, or want it as a longevity supplement: no trial shows that.
