**The short answer:** CoQ10's best evidence is in people with chronic heart failure: in the Q-SYMBIO trial, 420 patients on standard therapy had the primary long-term endpoint in 15% on CoQ10 vs 26% on placebo. Two meta-analyses on statin muscle pain disagree with each other, and we found no trial showing benefit in healthy people or proving ubiquinol beats ubiquinone. Talk to your doctor; do not stop a statin or heart medicine on your own.

## What the evidence shows

Grades follow the [rubric](/database#rubric).

| Question | Grade | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chronic heart failure | Moderate | [Q-SYMBIO](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jchf.2014.06.008): 420 patients, 300 mg/day ubiquinone for 2 years, primary long-term endpoint 15% vs 26% (HR 0.50); cardiovascular and all-cause mortality lower | Patients with heart failure on standard therapy; not healthy people |
| Statin muscle symptoms | Mixed | [2018 meta-analysis of 12 RCTs](https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.118.009835) (575 patients): less muscle pain, weakness and cramps, no change in creatine kinase. [2020 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2020.03.006) (321 patients): no benefit for myalgia or adherence | Small trials; the two reviews reach opposite conclusions |
| Total cholesterol | Emerging | A [dose-response meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgac585) found the largest reduction at 400 to 500 mg/day | A marker, not an outcome |
| Ubiquinol vs ubiquinone | Not shown | No head-to-head outcome trial found | Absorption differences do not equal clinical benefit |
