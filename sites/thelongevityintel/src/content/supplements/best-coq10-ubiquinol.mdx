**The short answer:** CoQ10's best evidence is in people with chronic heart failure: in the Q-SYMBIO trial, 420 patients on standard therapy had the primary long-term endpoint (major cardiovascular events over 2 years) in 15% on CoQ10 vs 26% on placebo, though the short-term endpoints at 16 weeks did not change significantly. Two meta-analyses on statin muscle pain disagree with each other, and we found no trial showing benefit in healthy people or proving ubiquinol beats ubiquinone. Talk to your doctor; do not stop a statin or heart medicine on your own.

## What the evidence shows

Grades follow the [rubric](/database#rubric).

| Question | Grade | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chronic heart failure | Moderate | [Q-SYMBIO](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jchf.2014.06.008): 420 patients with moderate to severe heart failure, 100 mg three times daily (300 mg/day) for 2 years, primary long-term endpoint 15% vs 26% (HR 0.50, CI 0.32 to 0.80); cardiovascular and all-cause mortality lower as secondary endpoints. No significant change in the 16-week endpoints | One trial; patients with heart failure on standard therapy, not healthy people |
| Statin muscle symptoms | Weak (contradictory) | [2018 meta-analysis of 12 RCTs](https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.118.009835) (575 patients): less muscle pain, weakness and cramps, no change in creatine kinase. [2020 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2020.03.006) (321 patients): no benefit for myalgia or adherence | Small trials; the two reviews reach opposite conclusions |
| Total cholesterol | Emerging | A [dose-response meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgac585) found the largest reduction at 400 to 500 mg/day | A marker, not an outcome; 50 RCTs, pooled total cholesterol fell 5.53 mg/dL (heterogeneity I-squared 70%), a small average change |
| Ubiquinol vs ubiquinone | Not shown | No head-to-head outcome trial found | Absorption differences do not equal clinical benefit |

The spread is eightfold. Q-SYMBIO's abstract names CoQ10 without stating the form; we did not read its full text, so treat the plain-CoQ10 and ubiquinone rows as the closest to the trial, not as exact matches; the ubiquinol products sell on absorption. Jarrow states that QH-absorb raised blood CoQ10 by 222% at 100 mg a day and 777% at 300 mg (its own claim, on its product page); a higher blood level is not the same as a better outcome, and we found no head-to-head trial of outcomes. The only NSF-certified product here is also among the dearest, so price and testing pull in opposite directions ([what each certification checks](/supplements/third-party-testing-supplements)).

The dose that produced the heart failure result costs far more than one label serving; that is a fact about the trial, not a target.
