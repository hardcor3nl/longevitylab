**The short answer:** creatine monohydrate is the best-supported supplement in our [graded ladder](/best/best-longevity-supplements) for building muscle in older adults who do resistance training, with a smaller memory signal and no evidence yet on lifespan. In a 2-year trial in older women it did not improve bone density. Talk to your doctor before starting, especially with kidney disease.

## What the evidence shows

Grades follow the [rubric](/database#rubric).

| Question | Grade | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exercise performance | Strong | The [ISSN position stand](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0173-z) concludes creatine is an effective ergogenic supplement. | Position stand, not a trial. |
| Muscle and strength in older adults with resistance training | Moderate | A [meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13061912) found creatine plus resistance training augmented lean tissue mass and strength vs placebo. | Large heterogeneity; some strength gains disappeared when loading-phase studies were excluded. |
| Memory | Moderate | A [meta-analysis of 8 RCTs](https://doi.org/10.1093/nutrit/nuac064): SMD 0.29 overall, 0.88 in adults 66 to 76, 0.03 in ages 11 to 31. | High heterogeneity (I-squared 66%). |
| Bone density | Weak | A [2-year RCT of 200 postmenopausal women with osteopenia](https://doi.org/10.1093/gerona/glz162) (3 g/day): no improvement in bone density or fractures, no extra lean mass. A [meta-analysis of 5 trials](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2018.00027) found no added BMD benefit with resistance training. | Few trials. |
| Kidney safety | Moderate | A [systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.jrn.2019.05.004) concluded no renal damage in the amounts and durations studied. | Short trials; people with existing kidney disease were not the target. |
| Hair loss | Weak | A [12-week RCT](https://doi.org/10.1080/15502783.2025.2495229) (45 men, 5 g/day): no group differences in hair or follicle outcomes. | Small, short. |
