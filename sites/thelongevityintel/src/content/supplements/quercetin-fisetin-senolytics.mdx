**The short answer:** the human senolytic trials we found used dasatinib, a cancer drug, plus quercetin, in patients with diabetic kidney disease, mild Alzheimer's disease or pulmonary fibrosis. They were small feasibility or pilot studies. We found no completed trial showing that over-the-counter quercetin or fisetin clears senescent cells or benefits healthy aging. Talk to your doctor; these compounds can interact with medicines.

## What the evidence shows

Grade: Not shown in healthy people (animal and pilot-stage evidence only).

| Trial | What it tested | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Hickson 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2019.08.069) | Dasatinib plus quercetin in people with diabetic kidney disease; preliminary report of decreased senescent cells | Preliminary, patients, a drug combination |
| [Gonzales 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02543-w) | Senolytic therapy in mild Alzheimer's disease, phase 1 feasibility trial | Feasibility, not efficacy |
| [Justice 2019, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis pilot](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2018.12.052) | First-in-human open-label pilot | Open-label, small |

None of these tested fisetin, or quercetin alone, in healthy adults. Where it sits: [graded ladder](/best/best-longevity-supplements).
