**The short answer:** the Pod 5 is Eight Sleep's previous-generation temperature-controlled mattress cover; Eight Sleep's Pod 5 web page now shows the Pod 6, and its store still lists Pod 5 Core at $2,799 (Full), $2,999 (Queen) and $3,199 (King), on 2 Oct 2026. Autopilot, a $199, $299 or $399 a year plan, is required for the first 12 months. The big sleep-improvement numbers come from Eight Sleep's own studies. Independent research shows bed temperature affects sleep but has not tested the Pod. This is general information, not medical advice.

## Pod 5 versus Pod 6

Eight Sleep's Pod page is now the Pod 6, so its current specs are not Pod 5 specs. A reviewer at The 5K Runner reports Pod 6 has about half the Hub volume, nine sensors per side (18 total), 550 W per side against 400 W, and reaches target temperature about 20% faster (the last is Eight Sleep's claim as relayed by the reviewer; [Sept 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/23/8sleep-eightsleep-pod-6-review/)). The same reviewer's Pod 5 write-up gives a 13 to 44 degrees C range and says the Pod 5 Hub is "too tall to fit under most bed frames". The reviewer discloses being an Eight Sleep affiliate who is gifted the product, so treat the review as relationship-tied, not independent. If you can get a Pod 5 at a discount, the question is whether faster response and extra sensors matter to you; no independent test of that exists.

## What the evidence says

| Claim | Who ran it | What it is | Our read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 34% more deep sleep, 44% less time to fall asleep, 32% higher sleep-quality scores | Eight Sleep (vendor-hosted) | "Based on Eight Sleep internal study data" per the Pod page; vendor describes over 50 in-house clinical studies | Vendor-run; no independent replication found; "up to" figures. Eight Sleep's site says the studies are "independently conducted" but also describes them as its in-house team's work, so we treat them as vendor-run |
| Heart rate and HRV improved over a week with the Pod on | Eight Sleep (vendor-hosted) | 54 participants, over 300 nights, one week off then one week on, home sleep test device, per Eight Sleep's description | Vendor-run, short, not blinded (participants know the Pod is on) |
| 56% fewer night-time hot flashes | Eight Sleep (vendor-hosted) | Clinical study n=98 menopausal women, Pod on versus off, per its footnote (another headline on its site says 55%) | Vendor-run; not peer-reviewed so far as we found |
| Most accurate sleep staging among trackers | A reviewer's 30-night comparison with WHOOP and Oura | Single-user consumer comparison, affiliate links, no polysomnography reference | Anecdotal consumer test, not validation |
| Heart rate "matching the gold standard" ECG, "99% precision" | Eight Sleep | Marketing statement on its Health Check section | Unsourced on the page |

Independent research is about temperature, not this product:

- A [2012 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22738673/) found heat exposure increases wakefulness and reduces slow-wave and REM sleep in real-life bedding conditions, and that cold exposure may matter more than assumed.
- A [2008 study in Brain](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18192289/) used a thermosuit to raise skin temperature by 0.4 degrees C, which suppressed night-time waking and shifted sleep to deeper stages, especially in older adults. That is warming, in a lab suit, not a cooling mattress.
- A [2018 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30509635/) says research on sleeping thermal microclimates is "scarce".
- A [2019 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31102877/) of 17 studies found a warm bath one to two hours before bed improved self-rated sleep quality and efficiency and shortened time to fall asleep. That shows body temperature changes matter, not that a bed does the same.

A PubMed search for Eight Sleep on 2 Oct 2026 returned no study of the Pod. Evidence grade: weak for the product's benefit; moderate for the idea that thermal environment affects sleep.

## Who it suits

Eight Sleep may suit a hot sleeper whose problem is bed temperature, who can afford about $3,300 to $3,900 over three years and values per-side control. Cheaper steps first: a cooler room and the basics in the [sleep hygiene protocol](/protocols/sleep-hygiene-protocol), and the [best sleep tools](/best/best-sleep-tools). If you want sleep data without a mattress, see [smart rings compared](/wearables/smart-rings-compared) and the [HRV guide](/diagnostics/hrv-guide). Persistent sleep problems need a clinician.
