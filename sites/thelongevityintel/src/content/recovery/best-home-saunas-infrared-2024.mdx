**The short answer:** the best sauna evidence is from a Finnish cohort of 2,315 men followed for about 21 years: those using a sauna 4 to 7 times a week had a much lower adjusted risk of sudden cardiac death than those using one once a week (HR 0.37), with similar associations for other cardiovascular and all-cause deaths. It is observational, in Finnish men, about traditional saunas. It does not show that a home infrared unit does the same. Infrared-specific trials are small: mostly short-term heart-failure studies. Home infrared saunas cost from $2,099 (Sunlighten Solo) to $5,099 to $5,799 (Sunlighten Signature and Amplify) and about $7,300 to $7,800 at retailers (Clearlight Sanctuary 2), with Sunlighten's mPulse quoted by consultant (checked 29 Sept 2026). Talk to your doctor before starting sauna use if you have heart disease, low blood pressure or take medicines that affect heat tolerance.

## What the evidence shows

Grade: Emerging (observational for traditional saunas; small trials for infrared).

| Question | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency and cardiovascular death | [Laukkanen 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2014.8187): 2,315 men, median 20.7 years; 2 to 3 sessions a week vs 1: SCD HR 0.78 (0.57 to 1.07); 4 to 7 sessions: HR 0.37 (0.18 to 0.75), P for trend 0.005. Sessions over 19 minutes vs under 11: HR 0.48 (0.31 to 0.75) | Observational; men who use saunas often may differ in other ways; traditional Finnish saunas |
| Infrared sauna in heart failure | [Meta-analysis of 9 studies](https://doi.org/10.1002/clc.23077) (infrared only): 15 minutes at 60 C, five times a week for 2 to 4 weeks was linked to lower BNP and better ejection fraction, with no effect on blood pressure | Patients, short-term; strength of evidence moderate to insufficient |
| Infrared sauna in healthy women | [Crossover trial, 10 women](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctim.2021.102798): infrared sauna raised body temperature by about 1 C but did not change blood pressure, arterial stiffness or HRV differently from rest or exercise | Tiny sample, single session |
| Infrared home units and longevity | Not shown | No claim made |

## Which should you pick

- **You want the evidence-backed habit, not a product:** use a gym or spa traditional sauna first; that is what the big cohort studied. See the [sauna protocol](/protocols/sauna-protocol).
- **You want infrared at home on a plug-in outlet:** check electrical needs before buying (Clearlight lists a 120 V, 20 A circuit) and read the warranty terms.
- **You want the cheapest infrared:** the Solo System, from $2,099, for one person lying down.

**Skip it if** you expect infrared to reproduce the Finnish results, you have heart disease or low blood pressure, are pregnant or take heat-sensitive medicines (ask your doctor), or cannot afford the running electricity and space. For cold and light options see [cold plunge evidence](/recovery/cold-plunge-ice-bath-review) and [red light evidence](/recovery/red-light-therapy-evidence).
