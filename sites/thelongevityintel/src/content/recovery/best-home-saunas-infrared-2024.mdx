**The short answer:** the best sauna evidence is from a Finnish cohort of 2,315 men followed for about 21 years: those using a sauna 4 to 7 times a week had a much lower adjusted risk of sudden cardiac death than those using one once a week (HR 0.37), with similar associations for other cardiovascular and all-cause deaths. It is observational, in Finnish men, about traditional saunas. It does not show that a home infrared unit does the same, and we could not verify product claims. Talk to your doctor before starting sauna use if you have heart disease, low blood pressure or take medicines that affect heat tolerance.

## What the evidence shows

Grade: Emerging (observational).

| Question | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency and cardiovascular death | [Laukkanen 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2014.8187): 2,315 men, median 20.7 years; 2 to 3 sessions a week vs 1: SCD HR 0.78 (0.57 to 1.07); 4 to 7 sessions: HR 0.37 (0.18 to 0.75), P for trend 0.005. Sessions over 19 minutes vs under 11: HR 0.48 (0.31 to 0.75) | Observational; men who use saunas often may differ in other ways; traditional Finnish saunas |
| Broader benefits | A [Mayo Clinic Proceedings review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mayocp.2018.04.008) of the evidence | Abstract not extracted |
| Infrared home units | Not verified | No claim made |

## What to check before buying any home sauna

- Heating type (traditional, infrared, tent), and whether the evidence you rely on applies to it.
- Independent measurements rather than brand claims for heat and electromagnetic fields; we found none for the products the site previously ranked, so we no longer rank them.
- Warranty, safety listing and total cost including installation and electricity.

The product reviews for [Sunlighten mPulse](/recovery/sunlighten-mpulse-sauna-review) and [Clearlight Sanctuary 2](/recovery/clearlight-sanctuary-2-review) have not yet had a sourcing pass and should be read as unreviewed.
