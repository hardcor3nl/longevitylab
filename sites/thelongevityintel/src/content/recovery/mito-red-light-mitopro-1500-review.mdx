**The short answer:** "MitoPRO 1500" now names a family of large panels, and the current model, the 1500X, costs $1,299 on Mito Red Light's site (29 Sept 2026). Mito quotes its irradiance two ways, over 160 and over 73 mW/cm2 at 6 inches, depending on measurement standard. A lab Mito commissioned measured 72.5 mW/cm2 peak, and an independent spectrometer review of the older 1500+ measured 76.5 mW/cm2 average against a claimed 170. If you only see the older $599 and 97 mW/cm2 numbers online, they do not match any current Mito page. Whether more light helps depends on the outcome: see [what the evidence says](/recovery/red-light-therapy-evidence).

## Which panel is which

The site's original page described a 1500 at $599. Mito's current [MitoPRO X page](https://mitoredlight.com/products/mitopro-x-series) lists the 1500X at $1,299, 10 by 43 inches, 25 lb, 445 W, six listed wavelengths (590, 630, 660, 810, 830 and 850 nm), a touchscreen, Bluetooth app control, a 3-year warranty, a 60-day money-back guarantee, FDA Class II registration, and IEC 60601-1-2 and 60601-2-83 listed. Mito states "no detectable EMF" at 6 inches or more and flicker-free operation; we did not verify either. The older MitoPRO 1500+ and its v2 update were the previous generation; smaller MitoPRO sizes and other series (MitoADAPT, Original) exist.

## The irradiance numbers, kept apart

| Source | What it says | Model | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mito product page | Over 160 and over 73 mW/cm2 at 6 inches | 1500X | Two figures, "professional / consumer" measurement standards per the page |
| Mito-commissioned lab ([LightLab International](https://mitoredlight.com/blogs/mito-red-blog/independent-third-party-testing), ISO/IEC 17025, 12 Sept 2025) | 72.5 mW/cm2 peak; 149.4 W total radiant power; grid sampled and averaged at 6 inches | 1500X | Commissioned by Mito; reports available from Mito on request |
| [Light Therapy Insiders](https://www.lighttherapyinsiders.com/mito-red-mitopro-1500-plus-review/) (Aug 2024, spectrometer) | Peak 87, average 76.5 mW/cm2 at 6 inches; 136 W total | 1500+ (earlier generation) | Reviewer says the brand's 170 mW/cm2 claim came from a solar meter and that the brand's own lab fluence works out near 57 mW/cm2 |

Reading across them: the panel delivers roughly 70 to 90 mW/cm2 on the lab and spectrometer measurements, about half of the higher headline claims. Joovv's Solo 3.0 measured lower and PlatinumLED's BioMax 900 higher in the same reviewer's data; see the [three-way comparison](/recovery/joovv-vs-mito-red-vs-platinumled).

## Who measured it

Both the 1500+ figures and the 170 mW/cm2 context come from one reviewer, Light Therapy Insiders, which is affiliate-tied: its Mito review states the reviewer receives a small commission on purchases through its links and discount codes. Mito's own lab report is commissioned by Mito. We found no measurement of Mito panels by a party with no commercial link to any brand.

## Value

The same reviewer listed the 1500+ at $1,169 in August 2024, which works out to about $15.3 per average mW/cm2 (our arithmetic, indicative only). For a more expensive, more polished ecosystem see the [Joovv Solo 3.0 review](/recovery/joovv-solo-3-review); for spec-checking any panel use the [panel roundup](/recovery/best-red-light-therapy-panels-2024).

## What red light does and does not do

Systematic reviews support light therapy for pattern hair loss and some skin conditions, find no strength gain in healthy people, and mostly test lasers or targeted devices rather than large panels. See [does red light therapy work](/recovery/red-light-therapy-evidence). Mito Red Light panels are sold on [Amazon](/go/mito-red-1500) as well as Mito's own site; Longevity Intel may earn a commission from the Amazon link.
