**The short answer:** compare panels on independently measured average irradiance at 6 inches, total power, wavelengths, price and warranty, not on the brand's headline figure. In the one set of spectrometer measurements we found covering all three big brands, the PlatinumLED BioMax 900 measured highest (90.3 mW/cm2 average), the Mito Red MitoPRO 1500+ next (76.5) and the Joovv Solo 3.0 lowest (59.2). Whether more light helps depends on the outcome: see [what the evidence shows](/recovery/red-light-therapy-evidence).

## The spec checklist

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Instrument behind the irradiance figure | Solar meters are built for sunlight; Joovv criticises rivals for using them, and reviewers report they can overstate LED output. A spectrometer or a lab report is better. |
| Peak or average, and distance | Peak is the hottest spot. Average across the panel face at a stated distance (6 inches is common) is closer to what the body receives. |
| Total radiant power (watts) | Lets you compare panels of different sizes. |
| Wavelengths | Check red and near-infrared bands; the trials pooled in one meta-analysis used about 655 to 905 nm. More bands is not shown to be better. |
| Who tested | Brand-commissioned lab, independent reviewer, or none. Note affiliate ties. |
| Regulatory and safety wording | Joovv cites Intertek and IEC 60601; Mito cites FDA Class II registration; check the exact model. |
| Warranty and returns | Brands state 2 to 3 years and 60-day returns; check restocking fees. |
| Price date | Panel prices move; reviewers' prices are often years old. |

## Choosing by what you need

- **You want the best-documented output for the money:** the BioMax 900 is the only one of the three with an independent average above 90 mW/cm2, though from one reviewer with an affiliate link to that brand.
- **You want to try red light without a large spend:** entry models start under $700, but their output is unmeasured; use the 60-day trial terms rather than the spec sheet.
- **You want app control and modular stacking:** the Joovv, at a higher price per measured output.
- **You have a specific goal:** check the [evidence by outcome](/recovery/red-light-therapy-evidence) first; hair loss and some skin conditions have support, strength gain in healthy people does not.

## What red light does

Systematic reviews support light therapy for pattern hair loss and find no strength gain in healthy people. A [2025 expert consensus](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2025.04.031) of 21 panellists calls photobiomodulation safe for adults and effective for androgenic alopecia, peripheral neuropathy, wound and pressure ulcers, diabetic foot ulcer pain and radiation dermatitis; some panellists disclosed ties to light-device companies, and the consensus covers medical and aesthetic devices, not home panels specifically. Details are in the [evidence by outcome](/recovery/red-light-therapy-evidence). For light and sleep timing see the [circadian light protocol](/protocols/light-therapy-circadian-protocol); a heat-based companion is covered in [home saunas](/recovery/best-home-saunas-infrared-2024).
