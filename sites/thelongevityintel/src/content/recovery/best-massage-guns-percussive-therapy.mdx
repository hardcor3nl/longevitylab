**The short answer:** A massage gun may help with soreness after hard training, but the evidence is small and short-term, and nothing shows one brand works better than another. Theragun's PRO (5th Gen) lists at $529.99, Hyperice's Hypervolt 3 Pro at $349 and Renpho's range at $59.99 to $119.99, all checked on 29 Sept 2026. If you want one, buy on price, weight, noise and how many attachments you will use, not on marketing horsepower. See the [Theragun PRO review](/recovery/theragun-pro-gen6-review) and [Hypervolt review](/recovery/hyperice-hypervolt-2-pro-review) for the two premium options.

## What the evidence says

- **Massage in general.** A [2017 meta-analysis of 11 trials](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2017.00747) (504 people, mostly manual massage) found massage after hard exercise lowered soreness ratings at 24, 48 and 72 hours (overall SMD -1.16) and slightly improved strength. It did not test massage guns.
- **Percussive massage.** A [2025 trial of 30 active men](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1561970) compared static stretching with 25 or 40 minutes of percussive massage after a soreness-inducing squat protocol; the 40-minute group had lower soreness and better jump measures at 48 hours than stretching. A [2026 trial of 37 men](https://doi.org/10.1002/pri.70246) found no significant strength difference from rest and called the benefits preliminary.
- **What that means.** Small trials in young men, short follow-up, no long-term outcomes. We found no trial showing a massage gun improves performance, prevents injury or extends healthspan.

## Who it suits, who should skip it

**Reasonable to buy if** you train hard, like the feel of massage, and see soreness as a nuisance a tool might ease. A cheaper gun is a fair test of whether you will use it.

**Skip it, or ask a clinician first, if** you have a bleeding or clotting disorder, take anticoagulants, or have pain, swelling or numbness that is not ordinary post-exercise soreness.
