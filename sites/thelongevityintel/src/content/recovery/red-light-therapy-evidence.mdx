**The short answer:** it depends on what you want it for. Systematic reviews support light therapy for androgenetic (pattern) hair loss and for some skin conditions, show improved fatigue recovery but no strength gain in healthy people, and a 2025 expert consensus calls it safe for adults. The catch: most trials used clinical lasers, caps or targeted devices at specific doses, not the whole-body panels sold for home use, so a panel's result is not guaranteed to match. Talk to your doctor for any medical condition.

## Grade by outcome

Grades follow the [rubric](/database#rubric). Each row summarises the review named, read from its PubMed abstract on 29 Sept 2026.

| Outcome | Grade | What the review found | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Androgenetic alopecia (pattern hair loss) | Moderate | [38 studies, 3,098 patients](https://doi.org/10.1097/DSS.0000000000004442): hair density rose vs placebo (SMD 1.14 under 20 weeks; 1.44 over 20 weeks). | Very high heterogeneity (I-squared 81 to 88%). Devices were low-level lasers and LEDs, mostly for the scalp. |
| Skin: acne and rejuvenation | Emerging to Moderate | A [meta-analysis of 31 studies](https://doi.org/10.1111/phpp.12841) found red plus blue LED reduced acne (SMD -2.42), and other LED and near-infrared treatments improved skin rejuvenation. | Wound healing and eczema results were highly heterogeneous. Wavelength and dose varied. |
| Muscle strength in healthy people | No support | [16 RCTs, 340 people](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10103-023-03956-2): no improvement in strength or functional capacity. | Trials varied in wavelength (655 to 905 nm), energy and timing. |
| Fatigue recovery and repetitions | Emerging | The same review found better fatigue recovery. A [2025 meta-analysis of 12 studies](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10103-025-04441-8) found a small overall gain in repetitions to failure (mean difference 3.87, CI 1.06 to 6.69), larger for the upper limb. | Small samples; acute effects when applied before exercise, not long-term adaptation. |
| Safety in adults | Moderate | The [2025 dermatology consensus](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2025.04.031) (21 experts, Delphi process): safe for adults; red light does not induce DNA damage. Effective for peripheral neuropathy, androgenetic alopecia, wound and pressure ulcers, diabetic foot ulcer pain and acute radiation dermatitis. | Expert consensus, not a trial; several panel members disclosed ties to light-device companies. |

## Why panels are not the same as the trials

The pooled trials applied light to a specific spot or region, often with a laser or a purpose-built LED device, and reported energy per point in joules. A home panel spreads light across a large area and its output is quoted in mW/cm2, usually measured in a way that varies by brand. Nobody has shown that a given panel at a given distance and time reproduces a trial's dose. That is why the [claimed versus independently measured irradiance](/recovery/joovv-vs-mito-red-vs-platinumled) matters: it tells you how much light a panel really delivers, though not whether that amount works.

Regulatory wording is also modest. Joovv's Solo 3.0 is described on Joovv's site as a Class II medical device intended for topical heating to temporarily relieve minor muscle, joint and minor arthritis pain and muscle spasms and temporarily increase local circulation, and states it is not intended to diagnose, cure or prevent specific diseases or conditions. Claims beyond that are not part of the stated indication.

## What this means for buying

- If pattern hair loss is the goal, look for a device designed for the scalp and ask a dermatologist; a body panel is not the tested product.
- If recovery is the goal, expect at most modest fatigue benefits, not strength gains.
- If you buy a panel anyway, compare them on measured output and price in the [three-way panel comparison](/recovery/joovv-vs-mito-red-vs-platinumled), or read individual reviews of the [Joovv Solo 3.0](/recovery/joovv-solo-3-review) and [Mito Red MitoPRO 1500](/recovery/mito-red-light-mitopro-1500-review). ## Method note

Researched from PubMed abstract records (read 29 Sept 2026) for five reviews and the Joovv product page. We read abstracts, not full texts, and did not test any device.
