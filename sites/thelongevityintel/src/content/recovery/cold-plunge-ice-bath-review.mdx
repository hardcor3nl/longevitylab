**The short answer:** cold water immersion helps with muscle soreness after hard exercise. A 2023 meta-analysis of 28 studies in physically active people found it better than other common recovery methods for soreness and similar for recovery of power and flexibility. It says nothing about longevity or general health, and a 2026 network meta-analysis of 87 studies found passive rest did best for strength gains from lifting. Home plunges cost roughly $5,600 to $12,500 (Plunge on sale to Morozko Forge, checked 29 Sept 2026), and cold water stresses the heart, so talk to your doctor first if you have any heart, blood pressure or circulation condition.

## What the evidence shows

| Question | Grade | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Muscle soreness after strenuous exercise | Moderate | [28 studies meta-analysed](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01800-1): cold water immersion superior to other methods for soreness; similar for power and flexibility; more effective than active recovery, contrast therapy and warm-water immersion for most outcomes; air cryotherapy better for strength | Active populations; short-term outcomes; temperature and exposure time rarely moderators |
| Strength and power adaptation to resistance training | Low | [87 studies, 2,313 people](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-026-01653-5): passive recovery ranked highest for strength; long, cold protocols showed possible power benefits with low certainty | 85% male participants; mostly indirect comparisons |
| Safety | n/a | [Physiology review](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2012.229864): submersion can trigger heart arrhythmias in healthy volunteers via simultaneous cold shock and diving responses | A hypothesis and review, not an incidence figure |
| Longevity, metabolic or mood benefits | Not shown | None verified | No claim made |

## Who it suits, who should skip it

**Reasonable to try if** you are a healthy, active adult who gets sore after hard sessions and will use it often enough to justify the space and cost. A cold shower is the cheap test; see [cold showers](/protocols/cold-shower-protocol) and the [cold exposure protocol](/protocols/cold-exposure-protocol).

**Skip it, or ask a doctor first, if** you have heart disease, high blood pressure, circulation problems or a history of arrhythmia; if strength gains are your main goal (see the network meta-analysis); or if you want a proven longevity tool. Heat is better studied for long-term outcomes: [sauna](/protocols/sauna-protocol).
