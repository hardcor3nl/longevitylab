**The short answer:** PEMF (pulsed electromagnetic field) therapy has cell-level research but weak clinical consensus. The 2024 systematic review update on acute bone fractures included only 3 randomised trials with 197 patients, found no significant effect of PEMF on the acute bone healing process, and concluded it does not support PEMF for improving fracture healing. We did not verify evidence for pain, sleep or longevity claims made for consumer mats. Talk to your doctor, and ask first if you have an implanted electronic device.

## What the evidence shows

Grade: Not established.

| Question | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Acute bone fractures | [Systematic review update 2024](https://doi.org/10.23736/S1973-9087.24.08226-1): 3 RCTs, 197 patients; none of the studies found a significant effect on acute bone healing; contradictory results on pain; one study showed a transitory gain in strength and range of motion | Small; clinical devices; abstract only |
| Pain, sleep, longevity (consumer devices) | Not assessed | No claim made |
