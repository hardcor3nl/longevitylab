**The short answer:** hyperbaric oxygen therapy is a medical treatment with FDA-cleared uses, not a longevity therapy. The FDA lists 13 cleared uses, including air or gas embolism, carbon monoxide poisoning, decompression sickness and thermal burns, and says HBOT has not been clinically proven to cure or treat cancer, autism or diabetes. We found no trial supporting anti-aging claims. Use it under a doctor's care, at an accredited facility, for cleared conditions.

## What the FDA says

From the FDA's consumer update ([Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy: Don't Be Misled](https://www.fda.gov/ForConsumers/ConsumerUpdates/ucm364687.htm), this text was read in search results on 29 Sept 2026 and the FDA page itself could not be opened; the wording dates from the FDA's 2013 update, so check the current FDA page before relying on the list):

- 13 uses of a hyperbaric chamber for HBOT have been cleared.
- HBOT has not been clinically proven to cure or be effective in the treatment of cancer, autism or diabetes.
- Consumers may wrongly believe devices are proven safe and effective for uncleared uses, and may delay or forgo proven medical therapies, or see no improvement or worse outcomes.
- In that 2013 update the FDA said it had received 27 complaints in the previous three years about centers promoting chambers for uncleared uses.
- It recommends HBOT under a doctor's care, in accredited facilities, only for cleared conditions.

## Where longevity claims stand

Grade: Not shown. We did not find a randomised trial of HBOT for healthy ageing, and a search for one returned no relevant results. Home "mild" chambers are a separate product class; this page does not evaluate them and makes no claim about them.
