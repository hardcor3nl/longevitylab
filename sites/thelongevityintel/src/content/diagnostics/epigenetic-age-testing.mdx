**The short answer:** if you want the broadest report, TruDiagnostic's TruAge is $499; if you want a cheaper methylation test, Elysium Index is $299 and Tally Health's TallyAge kit is $249; GlycanAge, from EUR 499, measures immune-system glycans instead of DNA methylation. None is proven to improve health, none diagnoses disease, and the clocks behind them can be noisy: a 2022 study found technical noise moved results by up to 9 years between replicates of six prominent clocks. Prices are from each brand's storefront on 29 Sept 2026.

## What the research supports

- **Reliability is the weak point.** [Higgins-Chen 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36277076/): technical noise produced deviations of up to 9 years between replicates for six prominent clocks; retrained principal-component versions kept most replicates within 1.5 years. The team's clock work is licensed by Elysium, which supplied some of the replicate data and did not fund the study.
- **DunedinPACE has published validation for pace of ageing.** [Belsky 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35029144/): high test-retest reliability, associated with morbidity, disability and mortality, and adding prediction beyond GrimAge in five datasets. Its authors are listed as inventors on a licensed invention.
- **Interventions show up on some clocks, not others.** In the [CALERIE trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37118425/) (220 adults, 25% calorie restriction for two years), DunedinPACE slowed while PhenoAge and GrimAge did not; effect sizes were small.
- **Glycan ageing is a separate signal.** IgG glycan patterns change with age and track inflammation and mortality in a [2026 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42025102/); that supports the idea, not GlycanAge's individual results.

We found no study that ran these four commercial products on the same people, so nobody can say which is more accurate.

## Should you buy one

Consider it as a curiosity or a rough trend line, not a health check. A gap between biological and chronological age is not a diagnosis, and a single result cannot tell you which habit to change; the [biomarkers that clinicians act on](/diagnostics/longevity-biomarkers-guide) (blood pressure, ApoB, glucose) and fitness measures such as [VO2 max](/diagnostics/vo2-max-testing-guide) have firmer links to outcomes. If you retest, use the same company and sample type, and expect noise. How one widely publicised longevity protocol uses these tests is covered in [this explainer](/protocols/bryan-johnson-biological-age-explained). Talk to your doctor about any result that worries you.
