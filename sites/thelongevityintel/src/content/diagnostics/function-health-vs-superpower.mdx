**The short answer:** the two memberships are close. Both sell two blood draws a year at Quest labs (or at home), with results in an app and a protocol or action plan. Function Health lists $365 a year for 160+ tests; Superpower lists $349 a year for 150+ biomarkers, both read on their own sites on 2 Oct 2026. Neither claims to replace a doctor, and in their own legal text both say they are not medical providers. Whether a broad panel helps a healthy adult is unproven (see the evidence section), so choose on price, location and how you want results explained. This is not medical advice.

Read the footnotes before paying: Function's FAQ says it charges "annually at $365", while its legal text calls the $365 the "first-year membership", so check the renewal price at checkout. Superpower's page says the membership stays active through the paid year and does not state a renewal price.

## What the doctor claims actually say

- **Function:** "Clinicians review every result and flag issues"; each result comes with explanations "created by top doctors", and Function names its Chief Medical Officer. In the same footer it says it is "not a laboratory or medical provider" and "does not offer medical advice". Urgent results are followed up by a healthcare provider, per the FAQ.
- **Superpower:** "Clinician-reviewed protocols" and "AI for everyday health questions. Real clinicians for added one-on-one support." Its terms say it "is a technology company" and "not a healthcare provider", and that content is "not tailored to you". It lists a medical advisory board on its site.

Read together: both attribute review to clinicians, and both disclaim medical advice in their terms. We cannot verify who reviews what or how long it takes, and we do not describe either as medically reviewed.

## Which tests overlap

Function says it goes beyond a standard lipid panel with "Apolipoprotein B, Lipoprotein A, and Lipoprotein Particle Size". Superpower's visible lists show categories such as hormones and thyroid (19 biomarkers), cancer and other risks (10, including PSA and hs-CRP), heart and metabolic (41) and aging (3, including a pace-of-aging and a biological-age test), but the page text we read did not name ApoB or Lp(a), so we cannot say whether it includes them. Compare marker lists on each company's own site before paying. Our [biomarkers guide](/diagnostics/longevity-biomarkers-guide) explains what the common markers measure, and [biological age tests](/diagnostics/epigenetic-age-testing) covers what the age scores can and cannot show.

## What the evidence says

Evidence grade: weak for outcome benefit, because no trial tests these products. The nearest evidence is a [2019 Cochrane review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30699470/) of randomised trials of general health checks in unselected adults: 15 trials with 251,891 participants reported outcomes and found little or no effect on total mortality (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.97 to 1.03, high certainty) or cancer mortality. Those were checkups, not consumer panels, and many markers they offer are useful in specific clinical settings. The practical point: a result outside the reference range needs a clinician to interpret it, and more markers also means more chances of an out-of-range flag. See [at-home blood tests](/diagnostics/best-at-home-blood-tests-2024) and [longevity labs](/best/best-longevity-labs) for other routes.

## Who each suits

- **Pick Function if** you are in one of its 48 states, want a single flat annual price with a stated mid-year retest and add-on imaging, and are comfortable that refunds are not offered.
- **Pick Superpower if** you want the lower list price, app-based protocols and wearable sync, and live in a state on its list (check the list first).
- **Pick neither if** you want a doctor to order and discuss tests: both disclaim medical advice.
