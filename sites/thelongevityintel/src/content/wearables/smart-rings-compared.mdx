**The short answer:** if you want a smart ring, Oura is the one the research covers best, and it costs about $615 over three years for a Ring 5 at the $399 US list price plus Oura's $5.99 a month membership (arithmetic: $399 plus 36 months at $5.99). Studies agree on what rings do well, sleep versus wake and overnight heart rate and HRV, and where they fall short, individual sleep stages and single nights. None shows that wearing a ring improves health. This is general information, not medical advice.

## Other smart rings

- **Samsung Galaxy Ring:** Samsung's US page lists it from $299.99 before trade-in credit and mentions no subscription fee; it needs a compatible Samsung Galaxy phone for full functionality, per the same page. We did not read a regular list price.
- **Ultrahuman:** its US page lists the Ring PRO at $479 as a pre-order shipping 10 October. Its global Ring AIR page lists $349 and states no recurring subscription fees for accessing data, but we could not confirm that price applies to US buyers.
- **RingConn:** its pages returned errors when we read them, so we give no price or subscription terms; check ringconn.com.
- **Patents:** Oura won a US trade commission ruling against Ultrahuman, with an exclusion order reported as taking effect in October 2025 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/ouras-itc-win-bars-rivals-smart-rings-on-proof-of-us-jobs), [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=535898)). The press also reports that Oura licensed its patents to RingConn in a settlement, and that Ultrahuman returned to US pre-orders in March 2026 with a redesigned ring that Customs ruled outside the order. We rely on press for this; check before buying from a non-Oura brand.
- **Validation:** our PubMed searches on 2 Oct 2026 for Galaxy Ring, RingConn and Ultrahuman validation studies against polysomnography or ECG found no study of those rings. That is not proof they are worse, only that independent evidence is missing.

## Validation studies, graded

Grade = how far the design supports the claim. All are device-versus-reference studies; none shows a health benefit.

| Study | Device | Reference, sample | Result | Grade and caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Robbins 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39460013/) | Oura Gen3 | Polysomnography, 35 adults, one night | Sleep versus wake sensitivity 95% or higher; stage sensitivity 76.0 to 79.5%; no significant difference from PSG for wake, light, deep or REM | Moderate: lab reference but one night; the first author is on Oura's medical advisory board and reports consulting fees from Oura |
| [Khan 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41230431/) | Oura (mixed generations) | PSG or actigraphy, 6 studies, n = 388 | No significant mean difference for total sleep time (-2.97 min, 95% CI -10.27 to 4.33), deep sleep (1.39 min) or REM (-3.89 min), but wide intervals | Moderate: meta-analysis of six mixed-design studies (PSG or actigraphy); averages hide individual error; no conflicts declared |
| [Herberger 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40108409/) | Oura, SleepOn, Circul | PSG, sleep-lab patients | Oura sleep/wake about 85% accuracy; four-stage accuracy 53.18%; authors say individual-level errors rule out clinical use | Moderate: patient population; the Circul ring did worse (about 65% sleep/wake); no competing interests declared |
| [Altini 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34201861/) | Oura ring data, own model | PSG, 106 people, 440 nights | 96% sleep/wake and 79% four-stage with the full model; 57% with accelerometer only | Weak to moderate: 5-fold cross-validation on one dataset; both authors are tied to Oura (one employed, one an advisor) |
| [Cao 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35040799/) | Oura | Chest ECG, 35 healthy adults, home nights | Accurate nocturnal heart rate and RMSSD; LF and LF:HF ratio had high error | Moderate: small, healthy adults, home nights; the authors rate only nocturnal HR and RMSSD accurate; no conflicts declared |
| [Physiol Rep 2025](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.70527) | Oura Gen 3 and Gen 4 | ECG, 13 adults, 536 nights | HRV concordance (Lin's CCC) 0.97 (Gen 3) and 0.99 (Gen 4), error 7.15% and 5.96%; highest agreement of five devices | Moderate: many nights but only 13 people; funded by a US Air Force research laboratory; abstract read on Crossref, as PubMed had no record |
| [Sensors 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36016077/) | Oura Gen 2 and five others | PSG, 53 adults | Sleep versus wake agreement 89% (kappa 0.51); sleep stages 61% (kappa 0.43); authors say all six devices need improvement for stages | Moderate: lab reference, one night, healthy adults averaging 25; the authors' research group receives WHOOP funding (WHOOP not involved in the study) |
| [Sleep Advances 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41768372/) | Oura and other devices | PSG, 13 young and 19 older adults, one night | In adults 56 to 80, Oura underestimated total sleep by 75.5 minutes and overestimated deep sleep by 71.5 minutes | Weak to moderate: small, age-specific |

Reading across: the evidence is good for sleep versus wake and for overnight heart rate and RMSSD, mixed for sleep stages (50 to 80% depending on method), and absent for readiness scores or temperature features. Accuracy for older adults and patients looks worse. Most studies name Gen 2, Gen 3 or Gen 4, so applying them to Ring 4 or 5 is an assumption. See the cross-device summary in [what the validation studies show across wearables](/wearables/wearable-accuracy-what-studies-show).

## Who it suits

**Pick a smart ring if** you want screenless overnight heart rate, HRV and sleep timing, and accept that stage percentages are rough. Compare the trade-offs with a band in [WHOOP vs Oura](/wearables/whoop-vs-oura-comparison), with a watch in [Apple Watch health features](/wearables/apple-watch-health-features), or against other wearables in [best wearables for longevity](/best/best-wearables-longevity-2024). For the number most rings headline, see the [HRV guide](/diagnostics/hrv-guide). **Skip it if** you want GPS or a screen, or reliable stage-by-stage data. Persistent sleep problems need a clinician, not a score; see [best sleep tools](/best/best-sleep-tools).
