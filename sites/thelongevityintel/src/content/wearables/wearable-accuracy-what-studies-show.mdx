**The short answer:** consumer wearables measure heart rate well, overnight HRV and sleep timing fairly, and sleep stages, calorie burn and VO2 max poorly. The best single comparison we found put five devices against an ECG for 536 nights in 13 adults: Oura's HRV agreed most closely, WHOOP 4.0's moderately, a Garmin Fenix 6's poorly. Nearly every study tested hardware that has since been replaced, in small groups, so treat these as guides to what each metric can do, not a ranking of current models. Nothing here diagnoses illness.

## Metric by metric

| Metric | What studies found | Study, size, limits |
|---|---|---|
| Heart rate (daytime, lab) | Accurate for most brands; Apple Watch and Garmin most accurate, Fitbit tended to underestimate | [Systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32897239/), 158 publications, evidence to May 2019 |
| Resting heart rate, overnight | Oura Gen 4 CCC 0.98 (1.94% error), Gen 3 0.97 (1.67%); WHOOP 4.0 0.91 (3.00%); Polar 0.86; Garmin excluded by the authors | [Physiol Rep 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40834291/), 13 adults, 536 nights, ECG reference |
| Overnight HRV | Oura Gen 4 0.99 (5.96% error); Gen 3 0.97 (7.15%); WHOOP 4.0 0.94 (8.17%); Garmin Fenix 6 0.87 (10.52%); Polar Grit X Pro 0.82 (16.32%) | Same study; authors report no competing interests |
| Total sleep time | WHOOP closest to the lab of three devices (Fitbit Charge 4, Garmin Vivosmart 4, WHOOP) in a review of 8 studies (-1.4 min); most wearables differed significantly from the lab in one 62-adult night | [Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38557808/); [Sleep Adv 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40303381/) |
| Sleep versus wake | About 86 to 89% agreement for WHOOP 3.0, Oura Gen 2 and others | [Sensors 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22166317), 53 adults |
| Sleep stages | Kappa 0.21 to 0.53 across six devices (Fitbit, Withings, Garmin Vivosmart 4, WHOOP 4.0, Apple Watch Series 8); Apple Watch highest at 0.53 | Sleep Adv 2025, 62 adults, one night; all devices detected over 90% of sleep but specificity was only 29 to 52% |
| Sleep in older adults | Oura under-read total sleep by about 75 minutes and over-read deep sleep by about 71 minutes in ages 56 to 80 | [Sleep Adv 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleepadvances/zpag006), 32 people |
| Calories (energy expenditure) | "No brand was accurate" (the review's words) | Fuller 2020 review, above |
| VO2 max | Apple Watch Series 10 under-read by 6.25 mL/kg/min, 13.2% error, right fitness category for 14% | [2026 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mcpdig.2026.100357), 40 healthy adults |

## What to take from it

- **Use trends, not single numbers.** Even the closest HRV device had about 6% average error against an ECG; day-to-day changes inside that band mean little.
- **Ignore stage percentages.** Sleep timing and duration are reasonably tracked; deep and REM figures are not reliable enough to act on.
- **Calories and VO2 max are estimates.** Direction of change over months is more defensible than the value.
- **Populations differ.** In an analysis of 1,536 studies, error was greater in studies with overweight samples, and greater for heart rate and calories in studies with more women ([Musa 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36535270/)); Oura's sleep readings drifted in older adults.
- **Recovery and readiness scores are not validated here.** We found no independent study showing that WHOOP Recovery, Oura Readiness or Body Battery predicts health outcomes.

## Limits of this page

Sample sizes are 13 to 62 adults, mostly healthy, and the hardware is one to three generations old (WHOOP 4.0 not 5.0; Oura Gen 3 and 4 not Ring 5). Some sleep-study authors report ties to device makers: the Sensors 2022 authors belong to a research group that receives support from WHOOP (WHOOP says it was not involved in that study), as the [WHOOP vs Oura comparison](/wearables/whoop-vs-oura-comparison) notes. Where an abstract gave no number, none is quoted here.
