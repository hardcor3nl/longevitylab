**The short answer:** Oura's ring is the strongest ring or watch we found evidence for on overnight heart rate and HRV. In a 2025 study of 13 adults over 536 nights against an ECG, Oura Gen 3 scored 0.97 concordance on HRV with 7.2% average error, ahead of WHOOP 4.0 (0.94) and a Garmin Fenix 6 (0.87). Its sleep stages are less reliable: like every consumer device tested, it agrees with sleep-lab staging only around 60% of the time. Oura's US membership is $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year (checked 29 Sept 2026), and the company now sells the Ring 5, so a Gen 3 is a used or older-stock purchase.

Membership, per Oura: $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year in the US, first month free for new members, HSA/FSA eligible. Without membership, Oura says the ring and app still work but insights and personal health data are "much more limited". So budget the ring plus $69.99 a year.

## What tests found

- **Overnight HRV and resting heart rate.** The [2025 Physiological Reports study](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.70527): Oura Gen 4 HRV concordance 0.99 (5.96% error), Gen 3 0.97 (7.15%), WHOOP 4.0 0.94 (8.17%), Garmin Fenix 6 0.87 (10.52%), Polar Grit X Pro 0.82. For resting heart rate, Oura Gen 3 had 1.67% error. Thirteen healthy adults, so results may not generalise.
- **Sleep versus wake, and stages.** In a [53-adult sleep-lab study](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22166317) the Oura Gen 2 matched polysomnography on sleep versus wake 89% of the time but on individual sleep stages 61% (kappa 0.43), second to the Somfit sleep sensor's 65% and ahead of the four other wearables (Apple Watch, Garmin, Polar and WHOOP, 50 to 60%), but still moderate. The study concluded devices are valid for sleep timing and duration but need improvement for stages.
- **Older adults.** A [2026 study](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleepadvances/zpag006) of 32 people found Oura under-read total sleep time by about 75 minutes in adults aged 56 to 80 and over-read deep sleep by about 71 minutes; the authors advise caution in older adults.

We found no independent study showing that Oura's Readiness score predicts health outcomes, and we make no claim about its temperature feature beyond Oura's description of it.

## Who it suits, who should skip it

**Pick an Oura if** you want the most accurate overnight HRV and heart rate we found evidence for, in a form you can wear in bed and while lifting, and you accept an annual membership. Read the trade-offs against WHOOP in [WHOOP vs Oura](/wearables/whoop-vs-oura-comparison).

**Skip it if** you want GPS or a screen ([Garmin Fenix 7 review](/wearables/garmin-fenix-7-review)), cleared ECG and sleep apnea alerts ([Apple Watch health features](/wearables/apple-watch-health-features)), or trustworthy sleep-stage percentages (nothing here provides them). Sleep problems that persist need a doctor, not a score. See [best sleep tools](/best/best-sleep-tools).
