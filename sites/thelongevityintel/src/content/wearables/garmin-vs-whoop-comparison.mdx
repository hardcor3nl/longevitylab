**The short answer:** Garmin's Fenix line is a GPS sports watch with health metrics and no subscription (fenix 9 from $999.99); WHOOP is a screenless recovery tracker sold as a membership ($199, $239 or $359 a year at its May 2025 launch). If you train outdoors and want maps, pace and a screen, buy the Garmin. If you want overnight recovery data on a comfortable band and do not need GPS, WHOOP. On the one head-to-head test we found against an ECG reference (13 adults, 536 nights), WHOOP 4.0's overnight HRV agreed better than a Garmin Fenix 6's, though both are older hardware. Neither device diagnoses anything.

## What the published tests show

- **Overnight HRV against ECG.** In a [2025 study](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.70527) of 13 healthy adults over 536 nights, WHOOP 4.0 had a concordance of 0.94 and 8.2% mean absolute percentage error; a Garmin Fenix 6 had 0.87 and 10.5%, which the authors called poor. Oura Ring generations scored best. Small sample, previous-generation hardware.
- **Sleep stages against polysomnography.** In a [53-person sleep-lab study](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22166317) a WHOOP 3.0 scored 60% agreement across sleep stages and a Garmin Forerunner 245 scored 50%; both matched sleep versus wake at 86 to 89%. Both are fine for sleep duration, weak for stage detail.
- **Recovery scores.** We found no independent study showing that Body Battery or WHOOP Recovery predicts health outcomes; both are proprietary. Use them as your own trend line.

## Who should pick which

**Garmin if** you run, cycle or hike and want GPS, a display, and to avoid ongoing fees. Detail in the [Garmin Fenix 7 review](/wearables/garmin-fenix-7-review).

**WHOOP if** you want a low-profile band for sleep and recovery and accept a yearly fee. Detail in the [WHOOP 5 review](/wearables/whoop-4-review) and against a ring in [WHOOP vs Oura](/wearables/whoop-vs-oura-comparison).

**Skip both if** you want cleared heart notifications (see [Apple Watch health features](/wearables/apple-watch-health-features)).
