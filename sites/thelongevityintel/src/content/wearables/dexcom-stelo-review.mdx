**The short answer:** Stelo is Dexcom's over-the-counter continuous glucose monitor for people not on insulin. Stelo's site (29 Sept 2026) shows a monthly subscription at $89 (struck from $99), sensors worn up to 15 days, Apple Watch support, and an app with coaching. The FDA cleared it for ages 2 and up not on insulin on 12 June 2026. An independent study of over-the-counter CGMs found 12.9% average error, so use it for patterns, not exact values. Nothing shows a health benefit in healthy people, and it does not diagnose diabetes. Talk to your doctor.

## Facts from the brand and the FDA

- **Who it is for:** people 18 and older not on insulin, per Stelo's site; the FDA notice covers ages 2 and up not on insulin. Not for people with problematic hypoglycemia because it does not alert to it.
- **Clearance wording:** an over-the-counter integrated CGM (iCGM) to detect normal and low or high glucose levels.
- **Price and plans:** subscription shown at $89 (struck from $99); one-time purchase also offered; HSA/FSA eligible.
- **Wear:** up to 15 days; about 20% of sensors may not last the full 15 (brand footnote).
- **App:** tips and coaching, morning recaps and an AI Coach the brand says is not medical advice.

## What the studies say

- **Accuracy:** [Sept 2026 study](https://doi.org/10.1177/19322968261482150): 39 young adults without diabetes, OTC CGMs, MARD 12.9%, within-15%/15 mg/dL agreement 66.5%; agreement worse after sugary drinks; sensors agreed with trends but were less reliable for exact values.
- **Does it help:** [2024 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-024-01692-6) of 25 RCTs: modest benefits mostly in diabetes; no trial in healthy adults.
