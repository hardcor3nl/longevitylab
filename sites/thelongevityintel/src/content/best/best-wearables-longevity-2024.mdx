## Why Wearables Are Now Longevity Tools

The wearable revolution crossed a threshold in 2023. These are no longer step counters — they are continuous biomarker platforms that measure HRV, sleep architecture, core temperature, and glucose in real time.

The longevity data being collected by a WHOOP 4.0 or Oura Ring Gen 3 would have required a clinical sleep lab and a team of researchers to gather a decade ago. Now it happens passively, every night, for $6–30/month.

## How This Page Was Researched

This page is a research summary built from manufacturer specifications and published validation studies. We have not tested these devices ourselves, and the rankings reflect the published evidence and feature sets rather than our own measurements.

## The Full Rankings

### 1. WHOOP 4.0 — Best for Recovery

For longevity-focused individuals who train regularly, WHOOP 4.0 is built around daily HRV measurement and a recovery scoring system. Independent validation of its recovery score is limited, so treat it as a directional guide.

WHOOP uses a membership model; compare the current plan terms and evidence limits before buying. [Read our WHOOP 5 evidence review](/wearables/whoop-4-review).

### 2. Oura Ring Gen 3 — Best for Sleep

A sleep-focused ring tracker. Its finger-based PPG sensors estimate sleep stages, and published validation studies against polysomnography, the clinical reference standard, report moderate agreement on staging. Its continuous skin temperature sensor has been studied as an early indicator of illness, though results are not diagnostic.

At $5.99/month, it is one of the lower-priced premium subscriptions in the category. [Read our full Oura Ring review](/wearables/oura-ring-gen3-review).

### 3. Dexcom Stelo — Best Metabolic Insights

The first FDA-cleared CGM for non-diabetics. Its readings show glucose responses to food, exercise, and stress, which most other wearables do not measure.

Wear it for one 14-day cycle minimum. Many users find it useful for seeing how meals and habits affect their glucose. [Read our full Dexcom Stelo review](/wearables/dexcom-stelo-review).

### 4. Garmin Fenix 7 — Best for Athletes

If you run, cycle, hike, or need GPS — the Garmin Fenix 7 is a strong fit. HRV Status, Body Battery, VO2 max tracking, and 14-day battery with no subscription. [Read our full Garmin Fenix 7 review](/wearables/garmin-fenix-7-review).

## The Bottom Line

The right wearable depends on your primary longevity goal:
- **Recovery and training:** WHOOP 4.0
- **Sleep optimisation:** Oura Ring Gen 3
- **Metabolic health:** Dexcom Stelo (at least one 14-day cycle)
- **Athletic performance:** Garmin Fenix 7
- **Budget option:** Fitbit Charge 6

For most longevity-focused individuals, we recommend starting with Oura Ring — the $5.99/month subscription makes it the lowest-barrier entry into premium health tracking, and sleep quality is the single highest-impact health intervention available.
