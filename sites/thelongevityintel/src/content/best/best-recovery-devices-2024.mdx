**The short answer:** no recovery device is proven to extend life. The strongest long-term data is for sauna: in a Finnish cohort of 2,315 men, 4 to 7 sessions a week went with much lower sudden cardiac death than one (HR 0.37), but that is observational and about traditional saunas, not home infrared units. Cold water immersion and massage have meta-analyses showing less soreness after hard exercise; red light has outcome-specific evidence; compression garments help slightly; hyperbaric oxygen is a medical treatment. Prices we read range from $379 (Normatec Go) to $12,490 (Morozko Forge, shorter tub), checked 29 Sept 2026. If you buy one thing, buy the one you will use, and start with the cheapest test of it.

## How to choose

- **Your goal is heart and general health:** the sauna cohort is the strongest signal, so try a traditional sauna at a gym before buying an infrared cabin.
- **Your goal is less soreness after hard sessions:** massage, a foam roller or a massage gun and cold water are the tools with trials behind them; the cheapest is a $39.99 foam roller ([massage and bodywork](/recovery/massage-bodywork-recovery)).
- **You lift for strength:** the 2026 network meta-analysis found passive rest ranked best for strength gains after lifting, so skip cold immersion right after training.
- **Your goal is skin or hair:** red light has the most specific evidence; compare measured output, not brand claims.

## Who should skip which

Ask a doctor first about sauna or cold plunging if you have heart disease, blood pressure problems or take medicines that affect heat or circulation; about compression if you have clots or circulation problems; and about a massage gun if you take anticoagulants. Do not use any device to replace treatment for a condition. Compare wearables that track recovery in [best wearables for longevity](/best/best-wearables-longevity-2024).
