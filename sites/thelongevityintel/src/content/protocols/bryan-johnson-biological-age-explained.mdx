**The short answer:** Bryan Johnson's Blueprint routine is a mix of well-supported habits (exercise, sleep, fitness, moderate calorie restriction) and unproven extras (a large supplement stack, medical procedures). No independent trial shows that the combination extends human life, and the biological-age results he quotes are self-reported by a founder who sells related products. Talk to your doctor before copying any part of it.

## What he publishes

His own [protocol page](https://protocol.bryanjohnson.com), read 29 Sept 2026, describes a last meal at noon, an 8:30 pm bedtime, about 2,250 calories a day (which he describes as 10% calorie restriction), and roughly six hours of exercise a week (three strength sessions and three cardio sessions). It reports a skin age of 39 at age 48, a 2.6% telomere length increase and hsCRP below detectable levels. The daily schedule is laid out in the [Blueprint protocol breakdown](/protocols/bryan-johnson).

Third-party reports relayed by [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Johnson), which we did not check at source: he moved to anti-aging experimentation in 2021, Bloomberg reported a cost around $2 million a year in 2023, and he has since cut daily supplements from 111 to 30 (by March 2026). He has said he will not repeat the 2023 plasma transfusions; the FDA has said such transfusions have no proven benefit and may be harmful.

## Element by element

| Element | Best human evidence | How strong is it for what he claims? |
|---|---|---|
| Daily exercise, high fitness | A [cohort of 122,007 patients](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.3605): lowest fitness carried five times the mortality of elite fitness (HR 5.04). Observational. | Strong association, and among the best-supported habits for longevity. Association is not proof that raising fitness causes the difference. |
| Calorie restriction (about 10%) | [CALERIE](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-022-00357-y), 220 adults on 25% restriction for 2 years: slowed DunedinPACE, no significant change in PhenoAge or GrimAge, small effect sizes, post hoc analysis. | Emerging. His level (10%) is lower than the trial's (25%). |
| Noon eating cutoff (time-restricted eating) | An [RCT in 139 people with obesity](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2114833): time restriction did not beat ordinary calorie restriction for weight or metabolic markers. | Unproven as a longevity tool; any benefit in trials tracks calories eaten. |
| Large supplement stack | See the [graded supplement ladder](/best/best-longevity-supplements): most compounds have small or mixed human trials. | Weak to Emerging for most items, and untested as a combination. |
| Plasma exchange | No trial supports benefit in healthy people; he says he will not repeat it, and the FDA has warned against it. | Unsupported. |
| Biological-age results | Self-reported on his own channels. No independent verification found. | Not evidence of effect (see below). |

## What DunedinPACE can and cannot tell you

DunedinPACE is the most rigorously built of the epigenetic measures in circulation. It was developed from the long-running Dunedin birth cohort and, in the [eLife paper](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.73420), showed high test-retest reliability and an association with morbidity, disability and mortality. It reports a pace of aging relative to the study population, with 1.0 as the average pace. That is a population-level finding. It does not establish that one person changing their score changes their lifespan, and different epigenetic clocks often disagree on one sample. We could not find a DunedinPACE value on Johnson's protocol page on 29 Sept 2026, so any specific figure you see quoted should be treated as self-reported. See the [epigenetic age testing guide](/diagnostics/epigenetic-age-testing) for what each test measures.

## What is worth copying

The habits with the strongest evidence are the unglamorous ones: regular exercise that builds [cardiorespiratory fitness](/diagnostics/vo2-max-testing-guide), [sleep consistency](/protocols/sleep-hygiene-protocol), and not overeating. These cost nothing. If you want the stack, use the [cost per study dose table](/cost-per-dose) to see which compounds have a human trial behind a specific dose, and take your questions to a doctor.
